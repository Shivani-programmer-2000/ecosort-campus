Four streams, one rule

The Solid Waste Management Rules, 2026 have required four-stream segregation at source since 1 April 2026. It replaced the older wet and dry split.

Wet

What goes inKitchen scraps, fruit and vegetable peels, meat, flowers

Where it goesComposting or bio-methanation

Dry

What goes inPlastic, paper, metal, glass, wood, rubber

Where it goesA Material Recovery Facility for sorting and recycling

Sanitary

What goes inUsed diapers, sanitary pads, tampons, condoms

Where it goesWrapped securely and stored separately

Special care

What goes inPaint cans, bulbs, batteries, mercury thermometers, expired medicines

Where it goesDesignated collection centres

How an answer is made

EcoSort never answers from memory. Every answer is built from a passage it can point to, and three checks decide whether it answers at all.

  1. You type an item

    Email addresses and phone numbers are removed before anything else happens.

  2. Ambiguity check

    A used tissue or a greasy pizza box depends on its condition, so EcoSort does not guess.

  3. Retrieval

    The closest passages in the rules and the campus guide are found (RAG).

  4. Confidence check

    A weak match becomes "Not sure" instead of a confident mistake.

  5. Answer with source

    The stream, the reason, how to dispose of it, and where that came from.

This page runs the offline mode, which answers straight from the best passage. In the full application, an IBM Granite model writes the answer from those passages, and its stream must match the evidence or the answer becomes "Not sure".

Built with IBM Bob and IBM Granite

Bob builds it

IBM Bob is the AI development partner used while building the project: planning the architecture, drafting code and tests, writing documentation and reviewing for security. A person reads and approves every change.

Granite answers

At run time, the full application uses IBM Granite models through watsonx.ai or Ollama, with retrieval-augmented generation so answers stay tied to the rules. The source code, tests and evaluation are in the project repository.

Responsible by design

Fairness

Plain language, no login and no cost. The knowledge base is meant to be extended with items from many regions and households, then tested.

Transparency

Every answer shows its stream, reason and source. Open "How EcoSort got this" to see the passages and the match score.

Ethics

Advice only. It never monitors or penalises people, and hazardous or unclear cases go to a person at the campus sustainability desk.

Privacy

No accounts, no names. Personal details typed by mistake are stripped, and feedback stays on your device as two counters.

EcoSort Campus supports SDG 12, Responsible Consumption and Production, with SDG 11 and SDG 13 as secondary goals. It is a student project for the 1M1B, IBM SkillsBuild and AICTE AI for Sustainability Virtual Internship.
